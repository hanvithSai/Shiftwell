Deliverable 1 · Recommendation memo

Build one swap-rules engine this quarter; defer WhatsApp and the custom overtime build

To: CEO  ·  From: Product  ·  17 Oct 2026  ·  Decision needed: approve the sequence below. Engineering validates the rules-engine estimate next week.
Recommendation
  • Build one swap-rules engine. Every shift assignment (roster entry or swap) is checked for valid credentials, training and weekly hours: passes are approved instantly, exceptions go to the right person, unsafe ones are blocked. Two-week sprints from 19 Oct: S1 credential gate, S2–3 engine with auto-approval, S4 hours-cap rule, S5 rollout, S6 buffer.
  • Do not build: WhatsApp approvals, a custom UrbanMart branch, a full credential suite.
  • Why: the three requests are the same check on a shift assignment. Slow manual approval is what separates our weak accounts from our healthy ones. The three accounts that asked (CarePoint, FreshBowl, UrbanMart) hold ₹254L, 62% of ARR.
Terms: ₹L = lakh (₹100,000) of annual recurring revenue (ARR). S1–S6 = sprints 1 to 6. CS = Customer Success. h = hours.

Not building

  • WhatsApp approvals (3 sprints). Half of capacity to relocate the same approval; message cost is small (₹0.5L a year for FreshBowl). If FreshBowl makes it a renewal condition, a notification-only version comes next.
  • A custom UrbanMart build (2 sprints). Single-customer code at about twice the cost of the same rule as a setting.
  • A credential suite (uploads, verification) or a regional hierarchy. We add one "approver" role instead.

Assumptions

Deliverable 3 · every assumption made where the data pack was silent
  • Capacity and calendar. Six sprints is the team's total, including testing. Sprints are two weeks from 19 Oct, so S5 ends 25 Dec and S6 (buffer) ends 8 Jan. Renewal dates are given as months; I plot them mid-month.
  • Engine estimate. About 2 sprints (range 1.5 to 3); the hours-cap rule about 1 on top. Mine, to be validated by the engineering lead.
  • S1 scope. The given 1 sprint covers the credential field and the scheduling block. Blocking swaps at approval, expiry alerts and a basic override log are assumed to fit; if not, they move into S2. Automatic swap blocking arrives with the engine.
  • Credential data. Customer Success loads CarePoint's expiry dates from their Excel; no import screen in v1. After 14 days warn-only, a missing required credential blocks. Unknown training goes to the manager.
  • Hours rule. Week is Monday to Sunday, scheduled hours, leave excluded. v1 covers swaps and extra shifts; roster building shows a warning. Approval at roster publish is a stretch item, so UrbanMart's literal "any shift" ask is partly staged.
  • Regional approver. No such role exists. I assume one "approver" designation set by Admin, built in S4.
  • Auto-approval. About 80% of FreshBowl swaps are eligible (the manager "approves almost all"). The 85% target is a hypothesis that exceptions keep today's pace.
  • Volumes. "25–30 a night" is 34 times the chain average of 24 per location per month, so treated as an outlier. At average volume, approvals take about 2–4 h a month at 5–10 minutes each. The benefit is speed and safety, not manager hours.
  • Off-app swaps. The line cook says half. Recorded counts therefore understate demand; I have not sized it.
  • WhatsApp cost. Four messages per swap at ₹0.35 is about ₹4,000 a month (₹0.5L a year) for FreshBowl.
  • Causality. Health labels and approval rates are as given. Twelve accounts show a pattern, not proof.
  • Certificates beyond clinics (diagnostics, gyms, some restaurant roles) are an inference. Clinics and diagnostics are ₹90L, 22% of ARR on 10% of locations.
  • UrbanMart threat. "Look at options" is read as a threat to cancel and move to another app, as relayed in the Sales memo. I assume an annual contract committed to August 2027, so the leverage is at renewal. No customer was contacted.
  • Churn evidence. Sales-recorded loss reasons are unverified; only GreenLeaf has a first-hand exit call.
  • Renewals before release. FitZone (Oct) and Brew & Co (Nov) renew before anything ships, so they are handled by Customer Success, not product.
  • Pricing. No price change or upsell is assumed; the engine is part of the core product.
  • Prototype scope. It checks only the person taking a shift; production checks both sides of a swap.
  • Churn base. Opening ARR is ₹408L plus ₹80L lost, so about 16% gross churn, ignoring new logos.

Build note

Deliverable 4 · words

The prototype is a single-file web app with no backend. It shows the new experience for four roles: staff and location manager on mobile, a regional approver, and Admin on the web. Pick FreshBowl, CarePoint or UrbanMart and follow a guided walkthrough: staff see who can take a shift before asking, the rules engine decides, and the right person is told. Managers see only exceptions. Admin edits rules, previews the effect on past requests, publishes, and overrides blocks with a logged reason. A release switch shows the product as of Sprint 1, 2–3 and 4+. A handoff tab holds the decision table, data model and open questions. It is plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript in one file, hosted on GitHub Pages; the rules engine is a single function.

AI: several tools, including ChatGPT and Claude, were used to cross-check the analysis, logic and code, alongside my own thinking and drafting. Chat links can't be shared; I'm happy to walk through the process.

A real version needs: a per-role credential model with expiry; versioned rules; weekly-hours logic (week boundary, leave, both sides of a swap); approver routing; decision and audit log; notifications; warn-only mode; feature flags and tests.
