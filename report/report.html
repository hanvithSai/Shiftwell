Shiftwell: what we build this quarter

To: CEO  ·  From: Product  ·  Date: 17 Oct 2026  ·  Decision needed: approve the plan below; Engineering validates one estimate next week
The call. Build one swap-rules engine instead of three features. It checks credentials, training and weekly hours whenever someone is put on a shift, approves what passes, escalates what doesn't and blocks what is unsafe. Ship the credential gate first (1 sprint), then the engine, then the hours-cap rule. Do not build WhatsApp approvals or a custom UrbanMart branch this quarter.

Approval speed is the pattern. All four current accounts under 45% of swaps approved in 24 hrs are at risk or on watch; all four accounts lost this year were at 60% or below (Fig. 1). ₹220L, 54% of ARR, is at risk or on watch and renews by March 2027. UrbanMart is the largest account (27% of ARR) but renews in ten months and is healthy.

Fig. 1. Approval speed vs account status. Bubble size = ARR. Source: data pack, Sept 2026. Directional, not causal: 12 accounts, and restaurants are structurally swap-heavy.

Sales' read of the churn is thin. Only GreenLeaf (₹16L of ₹80L lost) went to a WhatsApp competitor, and its former head of operations says it was not WhatsApp: managers couldn't keep up with approvals, and RotaPro let trained staff swap automatically.

The three requests are one mechanism. A certificate expiry, a 48-hour cap and "trained and no overtime" are all checks before someone is assigned a shift. Managers run those checks by hand today. WhatsApp is only a different screen for the same approval.

Two data flags. The FreshBowl manager's "25–30 requests a night" is about 30 times the chain average (2,900 swaps ÷ 120 locations ≈ 24 per location per month), so I treat it as an outlier store; the issue is latency, not volume. And off-app swaps mean recorded swap counts understate real demand.

The engine estimate is mine, not Engineering's: about 2 sprints (range 1.5 to 3), with the hours rule adding about 1. At 3 sprints the plan still fits with no buffer; beyond that, the hours rule ships as manager escalation only and WhatsApp is not discussed until Q1. The sales plan (3 + 2 + 1) also fills all six sprints, with no buffer, and shares no code (Fig. 3, last page).

What we will not build, and why

  • WhatsApp approvals (3 sprints). Half our capacity to move the same approval to another screen. Message cost is small (about ₹0.5L a year for FreshBowl); the sprints are the cost. Revisit at sprint 6; if FreshBowl makes WhatsApp a renewal condition, a notification-only version on top of the engine is the next step.
  • A custom UrbanMart build (2 sprints). Single-customer code, and about twice the cost of the same rule on the engine.
  • A full credential suite (document upload, verification) and a regional hierarchy. We add one "approver" designation and nothing more.

Assumptions

Deliverable 3 · every assumption made where the data pack was silent
  • Capacity. Six sprints is the team's total, including testing. Sprints are two weeks, starting 19 Oct, so "this quarter" runs to about 8 Jan.
  • Engine estimate. About 2 sprints (range 1.5 to 3); the hours-cap rule about 1 sprint on top. Both are my numbers, to be validated by the engineering lead next week.
  • Credential estimate. The given 1 sprint covers swap-time enforcement, the 30-day alert and the Admin override. If not, that work moves into the engine sprints, where swap checks live anyway.
  • Renewal dates. The pack gives months only; I plot them mid-month.
  • Hours. The week runs Monday to Sunday and counts scheduled hours; leave is excluded. UrbanMart to confirm.
  • Regional approver. No such role exists (Admins don't approve swaps). I assume one "approver" designation, set by Admin, reachable on mobile or web, inside sprint 4.
  • Overtime scope. v1 covers swaps and extra shifts. Roster building gets a warning to the manager; approval at roster publish is a stretch item.
  • Auto-approval share. About 80% of FreshBowl swaps are eligible (the manager "approves almost all"). The 85% target is a hypothesis; exceptions are assumed to keep today's 38% pace.
  • Outlier volume. "25–30 requests a night" is one store. Chain average is about 24 per location per month.
  • WhatsApp cost. Four messages per swap at ₹0.35 is about ₹1.40 per swap, or about ₹4,000 a month (₹0.5L a year) for FreshBowl.
  • Data quality. Health labels and approval rates are as given. Twelve accounts show a pattern, not proof.
  • Certificates beyond clinics. I assume diagnostics, gyms and some restaurant roles have mandatory certificates. The pack doesn't say; "not niche" is an inference. Clinics plus diagnostics are ₹90L (22% of ARR).
  • Credential data. After the 14-day warn-only period, a missing required credential blocks. Unknown training routes to the manager.
  • Interim UrbanMart report. Per-person weekly hours can be exported from existing data.
  • UrbanMart's threat. "Look at options" comes from the sales memo only. No customer was contacted.
  • Churn base. Opening ARR is ₹408L plus ₹80L lost, so about 16% gross churn.

Build note

Deliverable 4 · words

The prototype is a single HTML file with no backend. Pick FreshBowl, CarePoint or UrbanMart and it loads a synthetic night of 30 swap requests. Switch the credential gate, training check, hours cap, approver escalation and auto-approve on or off; every request gets a decision and a step-by-step trace. Credentials are checked against the shift date, not today. "Today's rules" reproduces the current product, where all 30 wait for the manager. Admin override of a block writes to an audit log.

The engine is one function, so it can move into tests. AI: several tools, including ChatGPT and Claude, were used to cross-check the analysis, logic and code, alongside my own thinking and drafting. Chat links can't be shared; I'm happy to walk through the process.

A real version needs from engineering: a per-role credential model with expiry; rule configuration per account; weekly-hours logic (week boundary, leave, both sides of a swap); approver designation and routing; a decision and audit log; notifications; warn-only mode and data backfill; feature flags and tests.
